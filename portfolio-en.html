    DevOps & Network Automation Internship - Inria

    From April to June 2025, I completed an 11-week internship at the Inria Centre at Université Côte d'Azur within the DIANA research team. My work was carried out in the context of SLICES-RI, a European research infrastructure dedicated to experimentation in networking and distributed systems.

    The first part of my internship involved experimenting with the FABRIC testbed. I created network environments across several sites and performed connectivity and performance tests using tools such as iperf. I then automated the regular execution of these tests using a GitLab CI/CD pipeline and a dedicated runner.

    The main assignment of my internship focused on developing a network configuration automation tool. Starting from a topology described in a DOT file, the tool analyses connections between devices and automatically generates the required VLAN configurations.

    I used Python and NetworkX to represent and analyse network topologies, then developed configuration generation for different types of network equipment. I also explored deployment automation with Ansible to reduce manual intervention and configuration errors.

    This internship allowed me to apply my networking skills while discovering DevOps practices for research infrastructures, including automation, CI/CD, reproducible testing and configuration management.

    DevOps & Research Infrastructure Apprenticeship - Inria

    From September 2025 to September 2026, I completed the third year of my Bachelor's degree in Networking & Telecommunications as an apprentice at the Inria Centre at Université Côte d'Azur within the DIANA research team. I worked mainly on the European SLICES-RI research infrastructure.

    The first part of my work focused on automating the management and description of experimental resources. I worked with POS and MRS to reserve machines, automate their preparation and collect hardware information such as processors, memory, storage and network interfaces.

    I developed and improved scripts that automatically transformed this information into structured descriptions usable by SLICES-RI. The goal was to make the resource inventory more reliable and simplify integration into the infrastructure.

    I also contributed to a performance study of an NVIDIA H100 NVL GPU. I set up several benchmarks to evaluate its performance, including experiments with MIG technology and language models such as Mistral-7B. This work included test automation, result collection and analysis.

    Scientific publications and research contributions

    My work on resource discovery and metadata management within SLICES-RI contributed to the scientific paper “A Data Management Infrastructure for Reproducible Experimental Research”, of which I am a co-author. The paper was published as part of the 29th edition of the ICIN conference in 2026.

    View the ICIN 2026 publication

    The experiments conducted on the NVIDIA H100 GPU also formed the basis of a second scientific paper, “Characterizing the Impact of GPU Frequency and Numerical Precision on NVIDIA MIG Performance”. I contributed to the benchmark pipeline, experimental campaigns, result collection and analysis, as well as to the preparation of the manuscript. The paper is currently in preparation.

    This apprenticeship strengthened my skills in Linux, Python, automation, distributed infrastructures, system administration and DevOps, while also giving me practical experience with experimental research and scientific publication.

  • Penetration Testing Techniques

    ×

    Penetration Testing Techniques

    During a penetration testing lab, I analysed and exploited security weaknesses in a simulated environment.

    Some of the vulnerabilities identified included:

    • Outdated software: an obsolete Samba service version allowed unauthorised access through a known vulnerability.

    • Lack of monitoring: outbound connections were not monitored, making remote control easier.

    • Misconfigurations: insecure scheduled tasks made it possible to maintain persistent access.

    • Lack of segmentation: other systems on the network could be reached from a compromised machine.

    This project allowed me to apply system analysis techniques, better understand security risks and propose appropriate remediation measures.

    As part of the SAÉ 4.1 Cybersecurity project, we designed Cyber Watch, a web platform intended to automate a cybersecurity audit and generate a clear report for the client.

  • GitOps with ArgoCD on Kubernetes

    ×

    GitOps with ArgoCD on Kubernetes

    In this research project, I explored the Kubernetes and GitOps ecosystem using ArgoCD.

    The goal was to understand how Git can act as the source of truth for automatically deploying applications to a Kubernetes cluster.

    I built an architecture using GitHub, GitHub Actions, ArgoCD and Kubernetes. Kubernetes manifests were stored in a Git repository, validated by a CI pipeline and then automatically synchronised by ArgoCD.

    The demonstration involved deploying an Nginx application and then changing the number of replicas directly from Git to illustrate the GitOps workflow.

    This project improved my understanding of Kubernetes, continuous deployment, the difference between CI and CD, change traceability and rollback through GitOps.
